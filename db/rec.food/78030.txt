	Confession time! As someone with more opinions than I can fit in <a href="https://smittenkitchen.com/books/">three cookbooks</a>, <a href="https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/778224/smitten-kitchen-keepers-a-kitchen-counter-conversation-by-deb-perelman/">one audiobook</a>, and even 18 years of <a href="https://smittenkitchen.com/recipes/">archives</a> on this website, sometimes when I want to grumble about something food-related but it&#8217;s not the time or place, I tuck it in a little document called &#8220;rants&#8221; that is so full of cringe, you only have my permission to publish posthumously. But I can&#8217;t write a headnote for this particular cake without first owning up to #23 on the list in advance: &#8220;Apple cider cakes are lies.&#8221; Which begs the questions: <em>Who hurt you, Deb? What gives?</em> Essentially, my quibble is that you can put all of the wonderful fresh-pressed apple cider you want in a cake, but it rarely comes through to actually taste like apple cider. The flavor is not robust enough. I&#8217;m not saying it can&#8217;t be wildly delicious with all of the cinnamon spice we also put in these cakes, but it rarely, to me, tastes like an accurate representation of its name.</p>
