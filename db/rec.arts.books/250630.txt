The Ohio-based indie publisher and &#8220;family outfit&#8221; turns twenty this year, and we at Lit Hub want to extend a hearty happy birthday.</p>
<p>In a literary landscape that&#8217;s often knocked for a fear of risk-taking, <a href="https://twodollarradio.com/">Two Dollar Radio</a> regularly champions under-sung weirdos and audacious stylists. They publish literary genre fiction, and askew memoir. Their slate favors voice-driven, totally brazen books that chronicle breakdowns—some solo, some systemic.</p>
<p>In honor of their twenty years of doing business, here are ten titles from the Two Dollar backlist that deserve a place on your radar. (Note: there&#8217;s a lot to love there, so consider this merely <em>a place to star</em>t!) Happy birthday, TDR. And long live <a href="https://lithub.com/tag/small-press-books-we-love/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">the indie press</a>!</p>

<p style="text-align: center;"><strong>Hanif Abdurraqib, <a href="https://bookshop.org/a/132/9781953387448"><em>They Can&#8217;t Kill Us Until They Kill Us</em></a></strong></p>
<p>If the multivalent Hanif Abdurraqib isn&#8217;t already sitting pride of place on your nightstand, I am here to tell you: <em>get wise</em>. This stunning collection of autobiography-infused music criticism is heart-forward and quietly huge. I&#8217;ve given this one as a gift more times than I can count.</p>
<p><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" data-attachment-id="242422" data-permalink="https://lithub.com/us-fools/us-fools/" data-orig-file="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/us-fools.jpg" data-orig-size="363,500" data-comments-opened="1" data-image-meta="{&quot;aperture&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;credit&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;camera&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;caption&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;created_timestamp&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;copyright&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;focal_length&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;iso&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;shutter_speed&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;title&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;orientation&quot;:&quot;1&quot;}" data-image-title="us fools" data-image-description="" data-image-caption="" data-medium-file="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/us-fools-218x300.jpg" data-large-file="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/us-fools.jpg" class="size-medium wp-image-242422 aligncenter" src="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/us-fools-218x300.jpg" alt="" width="218" height="300" srcset="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/us-fools-218x300.jpg 218w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/us-fools-44x60.jpg 44w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/us-fools-36x50.jpg 36w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/us-fools.jpg 363w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 218px) 100vw, 218px" /></p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><strong>Nora Lange, <a href="https://bookshop.org/a/132/9781953387516"><em>Us Fools</em></a></strong></p>
<p>This debut novel was a high point of my 2024 reading. The story follows Bernie, a young girl growing up on a farm in Central Illinois during the mounting ag-crisis of the late 70s. As a beautifully told, voice-y look at the obligations of sisterhood, this one put me in mind of another favorite: <a href="https://bookshop.org/a/132/9781635574975"><em>All My Puny Sorrows</em></a>, by Miriam Toews.</p>
<p><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" data-attachment-id="138884" data-permalink="https://lithub.com/the-50-best-contemporary-novels-under-200-pages/the-orange-eats-creeps-2/" data-orig-file="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/the-orange-eats-creeps-2.jpg" data-orig-size="1200,1683" data-comments-opened="1" data-image-meta="{&quot;aperture&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;credit&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;camera&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;caption&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;created_timestamp&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;copyright&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;focal_length&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;iso&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;shutter_speed&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;title&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;orientation&quot;:&quot;0&quot;}" data-image-title="the-orange-eats-creeps-2" data-image-description="" data-image-caption="" data-medium-file="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/the-orange-eats-creeps-2-214x300.jpg" data-large-file="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/the-orange-eats-creeps-2-730x1024.jpg" class="size-medium wp-image-138884 aligncenter" src="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/the-orange-eats-creeps-2-214x300.jpg" alt="the-orange-eats-creeps-2" width="214" height="300" srcset="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/the-orange-eats-creeps-2-214x300.jpg 214w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/the-orange-eats-creeps-2-730x1024.jpg 730w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/the-orange-eats-creeps-2-768x1077.jpg 768w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/the-orange-eats-creeps-2-43x60.jpg 43w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/the-orange-eats-creeps-2-1095x1536.jpg 1095w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/the-orange-eats-creeps-2.jpg 1200w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 214px) 100vw, 214px" /></p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><b>Grace Krilanovich, <a href="https://bookshop.org/a/132/9781953387509"><em>The Orange Eats Creeps</em></a></b></p>
<p>This madcap novel from 2011 has been called &#8220;<a href="https://www.bookforum.com/print/1704/grace-krilanovich-s-the-orange-eats-creeps-6695">beautiful and deranged</a>&#8221; by better minds than mine. Following a teen vampire who traipses through a 90s Pacific Northwest on a quest for a missing sister, this book is like Dark Twilight. But, you know. Punk rock.</p>
<p><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" data-attachment-id="250632" data-permalink="https://lithub.com/two-dollar-radio-turns-twenty-this-year-heres-where-to-start-with-their-radical-backlist/download-142/" data-orig-file="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/download.jpg" data-orig-size="192,262" data-comments-opened="1" data-image-meta="{&quot;aperture&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;credit&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;camera&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;caption&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;created_timestamp&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;copyright&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;focal_length&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;iso&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;shutter_speed&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;title&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;orientation&quot;:&quot;0&quot;}" data-image-title="download" data-image-description="" data-image-caption="" data-medium-file="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/download.jpg" data-large-file="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/download.jpg" class="size-full wp-image-250632 aligncenter" src="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/download.jpg" alt="" width="192" height="262" srcset="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/download.jpg 192w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/download-44x60.jpg 44w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/download-37x50.jpg 37w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 192px) 100vw, 192px" /></p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><strong>Sean Avery Medlin, <a href="https://bookshop.org/a/132/9781953387066"><em>808s &amp; Otherworlds</em></a></strong></p>
<p>One more reason to love Two Dollar Radio? Its commitment to hybrid, hard-to-classify works of cultural interrogation. This motley collection of liner notes, prose poems, and praise songs is an ebullient entry into that canon. In these pages Medlin, a multimedia storyteller and performer, concocts &#8220;<a href="https://twodollarradio.com/collections/all-books/products/808s-and-otherworlds">a speculative reality where Blackfolk are simultaneously superhuman and dehumanized</a>.&#8221;</p>
<p><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" data-attachment-id="250634" data-permalink="https://lithub.com/two-dollar-radio-turns-twenty-this-year-heres-where-to-start-with-their-radical-backlist/alligator-and-other-stories-cover-final-alzayat_grande/" data-orig-file="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Alligator-and-other-stories-cover-final-Alzayat_grande.jpeg" data-orig-size="443,600" data-comments-opened="1" data-image-meta="{&quot;aperture&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;credit&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;camera&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;caption&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;created_timestamp&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;copyright&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;focal_length&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;iso&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;shutter_speed&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;title&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;orientation&quot;:&quot;1&quot;}" data-image-title="Alligator-and-other-stories-cover-final-Alzayat_grande" data-image-description="" data-image-caption="" data-medium-file="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Alligator-and-other-stories-cover-final-Alzayat_grande-222x300.jpeg" data-large-file="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Alligator-and-other-stories-cover-final-Alzayat_grande.jpeg" class="size-medium wp-image-250634 aligncenter" src="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Alligator-and-other-stories-cover-final-Alzayat_grande-222x300.jpeg" alt="" width="222" height="300" srcset="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Alligator-and-other-stories-cover-final-Alzayat_grande-222x300.jpeg 222w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Alligator-and-other-stories-cover-final-Alzayat_grande-44x60.jpeg 44w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Alligator-and-other-stories-cover-final-Alzayat_grande-37x50.jpeg 37w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Alligator-and-other-stories-cover-final-Alzayat_grande.jpeg 443w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 222px) 100vw, 222px" /></p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><b>Dima Alzayat,<em> <a href="https://bookshop.org/a/132/9781937512897">Alligator and Other Stories</a></em></b></p>
<p>This debut story collection is currently high on my TBR pile. I remember being struck by the author&#8217;s bold POV in this <a href="https://theadroitjournal.org/issue-thirty-three/dima-alzayat-fiction/?utm_source=sendinblue&amp;utm_campaign=June_Updates&amp;utm_medium=email"><em>Adroit Journal</em></a> story, a few years back. Detailed, bracing, and highly attuned to the present moment, the pieces in <em>Alligator&#8230;</em>explore the psychology of &#8216;otherness.&#8217;</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" data-attachment-id="165307" data-permalink="https://lithub.com/everyday-horrors-life-at-the-intersection-of-art-and-death/attachment/9781953387004/" data-orig-file="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/9781953387004.jpg" data-orig-size="733,1000" data-comments-opened="1" data-image-meta="{&quot;aperture&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;credit&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;camera&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;caption&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;created_timestamp&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;copyright&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;focal_length&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;iso&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;shutter_speed&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;title&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;orientation&quot;:&quot;0&quot;}" data-image-title="Night Rooms: Essays by Gina Nutt" data-image-description="" data-image-caption="" data-medium-file="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/9781953387004-220x300.jpg" data-large-file="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/9781953387004.jpg" class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-165307" src="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/9781953387004-220x300.jpg" alt="Night Rooms: Essays by Gina Nutt" width="220" height="300" srcset="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/9781953387004-220x300.jpg 220w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/9781953387004-44x60.jpg 44w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/9781953387004-37x50.jpg 37w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/9781953387004.jpg 733w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 220px) 100vw, 220px" /></p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><strong>Gina Nutt, <em><a href="https://bookshop.org/a/132/9781953387004">Night Rooms</a></em></strong></p>
<p>I regularly look to TDR for its form-curious non-fiction. So here&#8217;s another essay collection that relates personal stories in surprising containers. In <em>Night Rooms</em>, Nutt considers her life in and alongside her favorite horror films, forming &#8220;<a href="https://www.npr.org/2021/03/23/980252396/in-night-rooms-a-life-is-reviewed-through-memories-and-movies">a narrative that explores identity, body image, fear, revenge, and angst</a>.&#8221; (As NPR&#8217;s Gabino Iglesias once put it.)</p>
<p><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" data-attachment-id="123113" data-permalink="https://lithub.com/ocean-vuong-the-10-books-i-needed-to-write-my-novel/crapalachia/" data-orig-file="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/crapalachia.jpg" data-orig-size="1034,1400" data-comments-opened="1" data-image-meta="{&quot;aperture&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;credit&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;camera&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;caption&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;created_timestamp&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;copyright&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;focal_length&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;iso&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;shutter_speed&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;title&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;orientation&quot;:&quot;0&quot;}" data-image-title="crapalachia" data-image-description="" data-image-caption="" data-medium-file="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/crapalachia-222x300.jpg" data-large-file="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/crapalachia-756x1024.jpg" class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-123113 aligncenter" src="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/crapalachia-222x300.jpg" alt="" width="222" height="300" srcset="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/crapalachia-222x300.jpg 222w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/crapalachia-768x1040.jpg 768w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/crapalachia-756x1024.jpg 756w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/crapalachia-44x60.jpg 44w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/crapalachia.jpg 1034w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 222px) 100vw, 222px" /></p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><strong>Scott McClanahan, <a href="https://bookshop.org/a/132/9781937512125"><em>Crapalachia</em></a></strong></p>
<p>This memoir of a rural West Virginia childhood was one of those books that got breathlessly passed around my circle for a while in the late aughts. <em>Crapalachia </em>stays beloved for its witty, stylish prose and palpable love of place. I&#8217;ve been meaning to revisit it, ever since that evil son of Appalachia rose to power on the wings of a much worse biography.</p>
<p><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" data-attachment-id="250635" data-permalink="https://lithub.com/two-dollar-radio-turns-twenty-this-year-heres-where-to-start-with-their-radical-backlist/81orz6c8zhl-_ac_uf10001000_ql80_/" data-orig-file="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/81OrZ6c8ZHL._AC_UF10001000_QL80_.jpg" data-orig-size="733,1000" data-comments-opened="1" data-image-meta="{&quot;aperture&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;credit&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;camera&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;caption&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;created_timestamp&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;copyright&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;focal_length&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;iso&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;shutter_speed&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;title&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;orientation&quot;:&quot;0&quot;}" data-image-title="81OrZ6c8ZHL._AC_UF1000,1000_QL80_" data-image-description="" data-image-caption="" data-medium-file="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/81OrZ6c8ZHL._AC_UF10001000_QL80_-220x300.jpg" data-large-file="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/81OrZ6c8ZHL._AC_UF10001000_QL80_.jpg" class="size-medium wp-image-250635 aligncenter" src="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/81OrZ6c8ZHL._AC_UF10001000_QL80_-220x300.jpg" alt="" width="220" height="300" srcset="https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/81OrZ6c8ZHL._AC_UF10001000_QL80_-220x300.jpg 220w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/81OrZ6c8ZHL._AC_UF10001000_QL80_-44x60.jpg 44w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/81OrZ6c8ZHL._AC_UF10001000_QL80_-37x50.jpg 37w, https://s26162.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/81OrZ6c8ZHL._AC_UF10001000_QL80_.jpg 733w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 220px) 100vw, 220px" /></p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><strong>Andre Perry,<em> <a href="https://bookshop.org/a/132/9781937512835">Some of Us Are Very Hungry Now</a></em></strong></p>
<p>The form-bending essays in <em>this</em> collection consider the paradox of identity. Perry takes on one of my favorite subjects—reconciling the art we love with an artist&#8217;s troubled politics, and/or a certain identity—in this <a href="https://www.theparisreview.org/blog/2019/11/25/the-whole-fucking-paradigm/">incisive piece</a>. Throughout the book, his writing is sly and perceptive.</p>

<p>This punch of a novel, much-feted on its debut in 2018, has Gothic overtones. From the first scene, it&#8217;s just <em>So Dark</em>. But a sneaky humor creeps in. Following two sisters who are shuttled between their troubled parents, this book explores how allegiances are formed in families. Walk, don&#8217;t run.</p>
