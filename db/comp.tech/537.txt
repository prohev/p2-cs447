<p>The smart home holds so much promise. It can make life more convenient with <a href="https://www.theverge.com/23156554/smart-bulbs-switch-lighting-guide-how-to">lights</a> that turn on as you walk in a room, doors that <a href="https://www.theverge.com/23393163/best-smart-door-lock">unlock as you approach</a>, and robots that <a href="https://www.theverge.com/22997597/best-robot-vacuum-cleaner">clean your floors</a>. It can also make your home safer, more energy efficient, and even a little more fun. (Have you tried asking Alexa to beam you up?)</p>

<p>But for all its benefits, the smart home can be complicated, confusing, and <a href="https://www.theverge.com/24009519/hue-bridge-matter-upgrade-problems">occasionally maddening</a>. It&rsquo;s also hard to keep up with all the changes. New gadgets are arriving daily, new features come to old products, and there are <em>so many</em> different ways to turn on a smart light bulb.</p>

<p>If you need a guide, that&rsquo;s what I&rsquo;m here for.</p>
